## 2. Reality check on the calendar

Today is 23 August 2026. Dhanteras is **6 November 2026** — 75 days away.

- China → Nhava Sheva or ICD Tughlakabad by sea: 18–28 days sailing, plus 7–15 days for production, plus 5–10 days customs. That is 45–55 days if nothing goes wrong. Chinese festive-light factories are booked solid from June to September and their lead times stretch to 30–40 days in this window.
- **So: you cannot import your Diwali 2026 jhalar/ladi/pixel stock from China. That ship has sailed.** Buy Diwali 2026 stock in India — from the importers in your own market, from Sadar Bazar, and from Ahmedabad/Rajkot.
- Use Diwali 2026 to *record* what sells. Which wattage, which CCT, which jhalar length, which chandelier price band. That sales record is what you put in a Chinese factory's RFQ in January.
- **Your China window for Diwali 2027 is April–June 2027.** Book samples by February, place the order by May, goods land August–September.

## 3. Before you can import anything

You cannot legally import without these. Get them now — they take 1–3 weeks, not months.

1. **GSTIN.** Your website still shows `GSTIN: __`. Without it, the 18% IGST you pay at customs is a dead cost instead of input credit you get back. This single line is worth more than any price negotiation.
2. **IEC (Import Export Code)** — DGFT, online, PAN-based, ~₹500, issued in 1–2 days. Permanent, no renewal.
3. **AD Code registration** — your bank gives an Authorised Dealer code letter, which your CHA registers at the port/ICD you will clear through. Without it your bill of entry will not file.
4. **A CHA (customs house agent) you trust.** Ask three shops in Bhagirath Palace who they use. Typical fee ₹8,000–₹15,000 per consignment plus port charges.
5. **Current account + a bank branch that does outward remittance (Form A1)** for supplier payments.

## 4. The compliance trap that catches every new importer

**BIS registration belongs to the Chinese factory, not to you.**

Under the Compulsory Registration Scheme, LED bulbs (IS 16102), LED luminaires (IS 10322 series) and decorative lighting chains / fairy lights / rope lights (IS 10322 Part 5 Sec 7) can only be imported if **that specific factory, for that specific model, holds a valid BIS R-number**, obtained through an Authorised Indian Representative and tested in a BIS-recognised Indian lab.

- The whole LED luminaire family was revised to the **IS 10322:2026** series and the concurrent-running transition ended **2 August 2026**. Anything registered only to the old standard is now a problem.
- If your supplier does not have the R-number, the goods can be held, rejected or destroyed **at your cost** at the port.
- **Therefore question #1 in every enquiry email is: do you hold a BIS CRS R-number for this model, and what is the number?** Get a scanned copy of the certificate before you pay a rupee. Verify the R-number on the BIS website yourself.
- Also applicable: **BEE star labelling** for LED bulbs, and MRP/importer-details labelling under Legal Metrology (importer name, address, month/year of import, MRP, country of origin) — printed on the retail carton, not stuck on later at the shop.

Accessories, drivers as spares, chandeliers/decorative fittings with no self-ballasted lamp, and profile/channel are treated differently from CRS-listed lamps — confirm each HS line with your CHA before ordering.

## 5. What a landed cost actually looks like

Formula (as your CHA will compute it):

```
CIF value    = (FOB price + sea freight + insurance) × exchange rate
Assessable value (AV) ≈ CIF
BCD          = 20% of AV                    ← HS 9405, LED luminaires
SWS          = 10% of BCD                   ← 2% of AV
IGST         = 18% of (AV + BCD + SWS)      ← recoverable if you have GSTIN
Landed cost  = AV + BCD + SWS + clearing + transport   (IGST separately, as credit)
```

Worked example — 5,000 pcs of a 9W LED bulb at USD 0.62 FOB Shenzhen, ₹88/USD:

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| FOB 5,000 × $0.62 | $3,100 = ₹2,72,800 |
| LCL freight + insurance (~3.5 CBM) | ≈ ₹31,000 |
| **CIF / assessable value** | **≈ ₹3,03,800** |
| BCD @20% | ₹60,760 |
| SWS @10% of BCD | ₹6,076 |
| CHA + port + ICD handling + transport to Chandni Chowk | ≈ ₹28,000 |
| **Landed cost (ex-IGST)** | **≈ ₹3,98,636 → ₹79.7 per bulb** |
| IGST @18% (input credit, needs GSTIN) | ₹66,412 |

**Rule of thumb: multiply the FOB rupee price by 1.32–1.45 to get your true landed cost** with a small LCL shipment. On a full container the multiplier drops to about 1.25.

Import only makes sense on a line where **the Delhi wholesale rate is at least 1.6× your landed cost**. Below that, the 60–90 days of blocked cash is not worth it. Check that per SKU, not per category.

Rates above are indicative for planning — confirm the exact BCD and IGST for each 8-digit HS code with your CHA on the day you file, and confirm 9405 vs 8539 classification (LED modules and lamps have been litigated into 8539 in some cases).

## 6. Tier 1 — cheaper buying inside India, starting now

**a) Your own market is full of importers.** Bhagirath Palace and the surrounding lanes hold some of the largest lighting importers in North India. Buying a 200-piece lot from a neighbour's container at importer+8% is cheaper than a bad direct import, and you pay in 30 days instead of 90.

**b) Go to the factory, skip the distributor.** For the technical range (panels, COB, battens, drivers, SMPS): the assembly cluster in **Noida / Greater Noida / Bahadurgarh / Sonipat**. For decorative, fancy, chandeliers and festive: **Ahmedabad, Rajkot and Morbi (Gujarat)** and **Aligarh** for fittings and hardware. Visit with a written list of your top 20 SKUs and monthly quantities — walk-in buyers with a printed list get factory rates.

**c) Take one or two brand distributorships** — Surya Roshni, Halonix, Syska, Wipro, Polycab, Havells, Orient, Bajaj. Margin is thinner (12–20%) but branded stock brings contractors and society/RWA tenders through the door, and those buyers then take your unbranded high-margin lines too.

**d) Use IndiaMART / TradeIndia / ExportersIndia properly.** Post a buy requirement with exact specs and quantities rather than browsing. You will get 15–30 quotes in 48 hours and can triangulate the real floor price. Never pay advance to an unverified IndiaMART seller — inspect or use a small trial order.

## 7. Tier 3 — how to actually start with China

1. **Shortlist 10–15 factories** (list in `02-china-supplier-shortlist.md`). Use Alibaba, Made-in-China and Global Sources, and filter for: 5+ years on platform, verified/audited supplier, ships to India, and — for lamps — an existing BIS R-number.
2. **Send the enquiry email** (`03-supplier-enquiry-email.md`). Expect 30–40% to reply. Chinese sales staff read short, structured English. Long paragraphs get ignored.
3. **Compare on landed cost per piece, never on FOB.** A factory quoting $0.58 with 25 pcs/carton and 0.09 CBM per carton can be more expensive delivered than one quoting $0.63 with tight packing.
4. **Sample first, always.** Pay for 3–5 samples by DHL/FedEx ($40–$90 freight). Test the driver, the lumen output, the heat after 4 hours, the solder joints, the wire gauge. Nothing else predicts a bad container as reliably as a bad sample.
5. **Trial order.** One SKU, 1–3 CBM, LCL. Learn the paperwork on a small loss-tolerant shipment.
6. **Pay 30% advance by T/T, 70% against a scanned Bill of Lading** — never 100% in advance to a new factory. Once you have a relationship, negotiate to 20/80 or to a 30-day L/C at sight.
7. **Inspect before the balance payment.** A third-party inspection (SGS, TÜV, QIMA, or a local Guzhen inspector) costs $150–$300 per man-day and has saved importers far more than that. Make it a written condition.
8. **Consider a sourcing agent for year one.** 3–5% commission, based in Guzhen or Foshan, who visits the factory, checks the loading and consolidates several factories into one container. On a first import this is cheap insurance.
