## Findings

**The gates passed and the detector still flagged it.** All eleven gates in
v3.0.1 were green on this draft. That is the condition the update protocol
calls "too loose", so four new gates were added rather than any existing
threshold moved. See the calibration table in `references/learning-log.md`.

**Colon-header bullets are the single most reliable tell found so far.** On
the MSME page every bullet shaped `- Label: expansion` was highlighted, while
the plain-sentence bullets sitting in the same list came back clean. The gate
is set at zero because the evidence is unanimous.

**A colon in prose behaves the same way.** "Aadhaar e-KYC is machine to
machine: an OTP or a fingerprint" got flagged; a 48-word sentence on the same
page containing a colon before a genuine list did not. The shape that flags is
a short declarative, a colon, then an appositive that restates it. The gate
measures colon density rather than trying to parse the difference, at 3.0 per
1,000 words.

**Semicolons welding two balanced clauses flagged on every page that used
them.** The working capital page scored lowest of the three at 23.1% and its
highlights were almost entirely this one shape.

**Four-item comma lists slipped past the rule-of-three regex.** That regex
requires exactly three items joined by "and". Document lists and eligibility
lists run to four or five, and they were highlighted consistently.

**Structure beats vocabulary at this length.** AI marker density was 0.0 per
1,000 words on all three drafts. Not one flagged span contained a word from
the vocabulary list. Everything that flagged was a sentence shape.

**List density correlates with the score.** The three pages ranked
41.6%, 37.9% and 23.1%, and their colon-bullet plus colon-expansion densities
ranked in the same order. List share alone did not correlate once colons were
controlled for, so it is reported as an advisory number rather than gated.
