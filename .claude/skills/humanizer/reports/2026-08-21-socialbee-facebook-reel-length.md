## Findings

**One pattern explains almost the whole 23.4%: parallel construction across
sentences.** Not the rule of three inside a sentence, which the skill already
catches, but two or three neighbouring sentences or list items built to the
same grammatical template.

Look at what got flagged in pairs and triads:

- Rows 9 to 11 are three list items of the form
  `<noun phrase>: <N> to <M> seconds, <qualifier>`. Identical shape, three
  times. The numerals "1.", "2.", "3." between them came back grey. The
  detector flagged the items and skipped the numbers, which is as clean a
  demonstration of shape-matching as you could ask for.
- Rows 12 and 13 both open `A <N>-second Reel that/stretched ...` and both run
  to a contrastive tail. They sit in different sections, three paragraphs
  apart, and both were flagged.
- Rows 2 and 3 are consecutive Key Takeaways bullets.
- Row 7 is `X covered A, and Y reported B` inside one sentence.
- Rows 16 and 17 pair `a 12-second hook for Tuesday and a 60-second demo for
  Thursday`, then `Preview each one ..., then drop it into ...`.

Every one of those was written deliberately, for readability. Parallel
structure is good prose style and it is also the single most predictable thing
a writer can do, which is exactly what a perplexity model rewards with a flag.

**Second finding: the flags cluster in the intro and in the product pitch.**
Row 1 is the entire 50-word short answer. Rows 15 to 17 are the SocialBee
section. This confirms the standing finding that intros flag hardest, and
extends it: promotional passages flag for the same reason. Both are the parts
of an article a writer is most likely to smooth into a template.

**Third finding: table rows get scored as prose.** Row 14 is three cells of the
specs table concatenated. `zerogpt_preflight.py` and `scan.py` both exclude
table rows from the prose sample, on the reasoning that a detector would not
weigh them as sentences. This report shows that reasoning was wrong for
ZeroGPT: it flattens the table and reads it. The exclusion is still right for
the *rhythm* statistics, since a two-word cell is not a sentence, but the cells
should be linted for marker density.

**What the pre-flight missed, and why.** All 11 gates passed and `scan.py`
reported 0.1/100 on text that came back 23.4%. Neither tool has any notion of
similarity between one sentence and the next. Every gate is either a
distribution over the whole document or a lexical check inside one sentence.
Two sentences can be individually perfect and still be a matched pair, and
nothing was looking.
