**The new pattern, and it explains most of the rest: statistic-dense
attribution.** Rows 7, 9, 11 and 16 are all the same shape. A named entity, a
date or a set of figures, and a reported finding, in one long declarative
sentence:

- "On 19 December 2025, Instagram's @Creators account announced that every post
  and Reel would be limited to five hashtags, down from the old thirty."
- "Metricool's 2026 Instagram Study looked at 24 million posts from 375,118
  accounts and found that posts carrying at least one hashtag averaged 31.7%
  fewer views and 33.89% fewer interactions than the platform average."

This is the encyclopedic register, and it is the most predictable prose there
is: once a sentence starts "Study X found that", the rest is nearly
determined. The bind is that this is also exactly what the E-E-A-T and GEO
checklists ask for. Specific, dated, sourced, citable. The fix cannot be to
remove the facts. It has to be to stop delivering them in one uninterrupted
declarative block: split the entity from the number, put a short reaction
between them, let a person appear in the sentence.

**Row 8 is a clause triad.** "It's a platform-enforced limit rather than a
suggestion, it applies to every account type, and splitting tags... won't buy
you more room." The existing `rule_of_three` regex matches noun-phrase lists
and walks straight past three coordinated clauses.

**What the pre-flight still cannot see.** Every gate passed twice in a row on
text that scored in the mid-twenties. The gates measure distribution and
per-sentence lexis. They have no notion of where in the document a sentence
sits, and no notion of a sentence's informational density.
