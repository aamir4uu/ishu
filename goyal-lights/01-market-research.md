# Bhagirath Palace Lights Business — Market Research

*Prepared 21 August 2026. Sources listed at the bottom; figures from third-party
research firms vary widely and are flagged where they disagree.*

---

## 1. The market you have opened in

Bhagirath Palace sits inside Chandni Chowk, Old Delhi (PIN 110006) and is
routinely described as **Asia's largest wholesale market for electrical and
electronic goods**. The lighting lanes alone carry **200+ shops**, running from
₹10 clay-diya-substitute LED tea lights up to imported chandeliers.

| Fact | Detail | Why it matters to you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trading hours | Roughly **11:00–19:00**, **closed Sunday** | Your Google hours must say this. Sunday is your content/photography day, not a lost day. |
| Nearest metro | Chandni Chowk (Yellow Line), Lal Qila (Violet Line) | Put walking directions on the website. Buyers genuinely get lost — the lane entrance is hidden behind a temple and a cinema hall. |
| Entry price points | Decorative stock starts near **₹10** | You cannot win on price. Everybody bottoms out at the same floor after bargaining. |
| Stock mix | Budget imports alongside Indian-made branded goods | Your positioning choice: be the shop that carries *both* and tells the buyer honestly which is which. |
| Competitor set | 200+ shops, several 30–40 years old (e.g. Seema Lites, est. 1985) | You are the newest shop in a market that runs on relationships. Discoverability is your only shortcut. |

### The single most important structural insight

In a lane where 200 shops sell near-identical stock at near-identical
post-bargain prices, **price is not a differentiator and neither is product**.
The things a new shop can actually win on are:

1. **Being findable before the buyer reaches the lane** — Google, WhatsApp, YouTube.
2. **Reply speed** — a rate quoted in 5 minutes on WhatsApp beats a rate quoted tomorrow.
3. **Transport reliability** — out-of-Delhi buyers care more about the bilty reaching Bareilly intact than about ₹2/piece.
4. **Replacement policy on LED failures** — the market's biggest unspoken complaint.
5. **Clean GST billing** — matters enormously to the retailer buying from you, and almost nobody markets it.

Every recommendation in `03-marketing-plan.md` is built on those five, not on price.

## 3. Who actually buys from you

You told me you want **both wholesale and retail**. Those are four distinct
buyers, and they need four different messages.

### A. Trade / wholesale (your revenue base, ~60–70% of turnover in a typical lane shop)

| Segment | What they buy | What they care about | Where to reach them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Retailers from tier-2/3 North India** (UP, Haryana, Punjab, Rajasthan, Bihar, Uttarakhand) | Mixed festive cartons, bulbs, batten, decorative | Rate list, credit terms, transport/bilty, packing | WhatsApp broadcast, IndiaMART, referrals |
| **Electricians & wiremen** | COB, panels, drivers, holders, strips, consumables | Availability, credit, small-margin kickback | In-person referral card, WhatsApp group |
| **Decorators / tent houses / event lighting** | Pixel strings, rope light, motif, DJ/party lights, ladi | Bulk stock depth, seasonal rental-grade quality | Wedding & Diwali season outreach, Instagram |
| **Contractors / shop-fitters / small builders** | COB spots, cove/profile light, panels, flood | Quantity consistency across a project, GST bill | Site visits, Google Search, JustDial |

Electricians are the **hidden distribution channel** of Indian lighting. The
householder does not choose the brand — the electrician does. A referral scheme
for electricians is the highest-ROI ₹0-media spend available to you.

### B. Retail walk-in (spiky, festival-driven, high margin)

- Delhi/NCR households doing Diwali decoration (Sept–Nov)
- Café, restaurant and salon owners doing interiors (year-round)
- Wedding shoppers and gifting (Nov–Feb, Apr–Jul)
- Temple, gurudwara, RWA and market-association committees (festival tenders)

Walk-in buyers arrive already primed by **YouTube market-tour videos**. That is
not a guess — "Bhagirath Palace light market" walkthrough videos with prices in
the title are a large, active content genre. That genre is your cheapest
customer-acquisition channel and it costs nothing but a phone and a Sunday.

## 4. Seasonality — plan cash and marketing around this curve

| Period | Demand driver | Product focus | Marketing posture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Aug–early Oct** | **Retailer stocking for Diwali** — the real wholesale peak | Festive strings, jhalar, ladi, LED diyas, motifs | Maximum push. Rate lists, broadcasts, reels. |
| **Mid Oct–8 Nov** | **Retail Diwali rush** (Dhanteras Fri 6 Nov, Diwali Sun 8 Nov 2026) | Everything festive + gifting | Fulfilment mode. Stop launching, start serving. |
| **Nov–Dec** | Christmas / New Year, café decor | Warm string lights, neon flex, star lights | Clearance of festive stock + café-lighting angle |
| **Nov–Feb, Apr–Jul** | Wedding season | Rental-grade decor, pixel, flood, motif gates | Decorator and tent-house outreach |
| **Mar–Jun** | Construction, renovation, interiors | COB, panel, cove, profile, fans | Contractor and electrician outreach |
| **Jan–Feb** | Lohri, Republic Day, Basant | Small decorative, flag/theme lighting | Low spend, content-only |

For a decorative-heavy lights shop, **Diwali quarter commonly carries 40–50% of
annual revenue**. Treat Aug–Nov as the year.

### Where you are standing right now

Today is **21 August 2026**. Diwali is **Sunday 8 November 2026**, Dhanteras
**Friday 6 November 2026** — **79 days**, about **11 weeks**.

The wholesale stocking window (when retailers from outside Delhi place their
Diwali orders) is **now through early October**. You have roughly **six weeks**
to be findable before the money in this market has already moved. That timing is
why `03-marketing-plan.md` is written as a dated 90-day sprint and not as a
generic annual plan.

## 5. Industry backdrop (use for confidence, not for forecasting)

Analyst estimates for Indian lighting diverge sharply — treat them as direction,
not as numbers to plan inventory against.

| Segment | Estimate | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| India lighting market | ~USD 7.4 bn by 2034, ~4.7% CAGR | IMARC / openPR |
| India **decorative** lighting | USD 1.7 bn (2025) → USD 3.0 bn (2034), ~5.9% CAGR | IMARC |
| India decorative lighting (alternative view) | ~9.8% CAGR 2026–2032 | 6Wresearch |
| India LED lighting | USD 11.56 bn (2025) → USD 18.8 bn (2031), 8.44% CAGR | Mordor Intelligence |
| India LED lighting (alternative view) | USD 6.0 bn (2025) → USD 27.81 bn (2034), 18.58% CAGR | IMARC |

The spread between USD 6 bn and USD 11.56 bn for the same year and the same
segment tells you these are modelled estimates with different scope definitions.
**Do not quote a single one of these as fact.** What is consistent across all of
them: decorative and festive lighting is growing, LED replacement is still
running, and festivals are named as a primary demand driver in every report.
