## What the new detector caught

The `parallel_construction` signal was added after the Facebook Reel article
came back 23.4% from ZeroGPT, where every highlight turned out to be one half
of a matched pair. It earned its place immediately here, on a draft I had
written without noticing either problem:

| Pair it found | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| "Instagram began enforcing that cap in December 2025" / "Instagram started enforcing that limit in December 2025" | Near-duplicate sentences in two different sections. A reader would have noticed; I had not. |
| "Adam Mosseri has said plainly that..." / "Adam Mosseri has said it about as directly as..." | Same construction, same subject, twelve paragraphs apart. |

Neither would have been caught by the opener-repetition gate, which compares
raw first words and sees "Instagram began" and "Instagram started" as
different. That is precisely the gap the report exposed.

## Contraction rate

Worth calling out because it nearly shipped. The first draft ran 5.51
contractions per 1,000 words, which **passes** the gate's floor of 4.0 and is
still wrong for this client: SocialBee's guidelines ask for contractions
throughout, and the Facebook Reel article runs at 22 per 1,000. A passing gate
is not the same as a correct draft. Final version: 22.05 per 1,000.

## Remaining honest tells

`scan.py` still reports `low_burstiness` at a sentence-length standard
deviation of 6.7. The authoritative gate disagrees: `zerogpt_preflight.py`
measures coefficient of variation at 0.572 against a 0.45 floor, comfortably
passing. The two use different measures and the gate script is the calibrated
one, so this is noted rather than chased.

The article also has no first-person voice and no anecdote, because the brief
specifies second person and a serious register. Correct for the client,
slightly flattening for a detector. A real tradeoff, not an oversight.

## The ZeroGPT run: 25.6%

Scored at **25.6% AI**, 1,159 words, verdict "Most Likely Human written, may
include parts generated by AI". Report filed at
`.claude/skills/humanizer/reports/2026-08-21-socialbee-instagram-hashtags.md`,
highlights extracted from the exported PDF by span colour.

All eleven gates had passed, `scan.py` said 3.9/100, and the scanner's recall
against the report was **0.0**: none of the 18 flagged sentences predicted.

### Parallelism was gone

Report 1 on the Facebook article was dominated by matched pairs and triads.
Not one highlighted span here is half of a pair. The detector built from that
report also caught two pairs in this draft before it was scored, and two more
that my own rewrite introduced afterwards. On ingest, `parallel_structure`'s
weight went **down**, from 16.6 to 14.25, because it stopped predicting
anything here. That is the calibration behaving correctly.

### What replaced it

**Statistic-dense attribution.** Four of the highlights are the same sentence
shape: a named source, a date or a pile of figures, and a reported finding, all
in one long declarative.

> Metricool's 2026 Instagram Study looked at 24 million posts from 375,118
> accounts and found that posts carrying at least one hashtag averaged 31.7%
> fewer views and 33.89% fewer interactions than the platform average.

That sentence is sourced, dated and specific, which is exactly what the E-E-A-T
and GEO checklist items ask for. It is also the most predictable prose in the
article. The fix cannot be to cut the fact, so the fix is delivery: the source,
then a short sentence, then the numbers.

Three other patterns hit a second sighting and are now measured: tables read as
prose, intros flagging hardest, and promotional passages flagging. The first two
had been standing findings for two versions with nothing measuring them.

### What changed

Four new detectors, all calibrated on ingest: `stat_dense` (lift 4.33, the
highest of any signal), `clause_triad`, `table_row_markers`, and
`intro_position`. Recall went **0.0 to 0.33**.

Then all 16 highlighted spans were rewritten. The opening was rewritten by hand
and the bolded "Short answer:" label dropped, since the learner promoted that
phrase after both reports flagged the block containing it. The AEO checklist
requires a 40-60 word answer at the top, not the label.

Two gates broke during the rewrite, which is the standing finding about
structural edits: mean clustering hit 42.9% against a 40% cap, and paragraph
variance fell to 0.339 against a 0.35 floor. Fixed by merging mid-length
sentences into long ones and cutting others to fragments.
