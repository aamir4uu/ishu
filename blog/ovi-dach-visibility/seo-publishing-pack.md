## Assumptions flagged

The topic was supplied. Keywords and key points were not (Task Info N/A), so:

- **Primary keyword set to "OVI index"** because it is in the supplied title.
  Search volume was not checked in Ahrefs or Semrush (checklist 1.1 open).
  "Visibility index" may be the higher-volume term; if so, the title tag and
  first sentence can carry both without rewriting the piece.
- **No proprietary OVI data was available to this draft.** xovi.com and
  knowledgebase.xovi.de were blocked from this environment, and the brief
  supplied no data set. The piece therefore rests on XOVI's publicly documented
  OVI methodology, XOVI's own published update analyses, and one external
  study. The "who wins" section describes patterns at the level of domain types
  and does not quote a single invented figure. **This is the biggest open
  item:** the article is titled as a data piece and will land far harder with
  two or three real OVI charts and numbers from the platform. See the section
  below on what to add.

## What to add from the real OVI data before publishing

Each of these has a natural slot in the copy:

1. In "Who Wins": the top five domains in the German OVI today versus 2015, as
   a short table. The sentence "Mostly, the names at the top in 2015 are still
   the names at the top in 2026" is a claim that needs that table behind it.
2. In "Who Loses": one anonymised affiliate curve stepping down through August
   2018 and June 2025, with the percentage drop at each step.
3. In "Why Rankings and Clicks Split": one domain where OVI stayed flat through
   2025 while Search Console clicks fell, with both numbers.
4. Optionally: how many of the 500,000 keywords sit in each industry bucket.

If the data cannot be supplied, the piece publishes as informed analysis and is
honest about that, but consider softening the title to "What the OVI Index
Shows About Who Wins and Why" without the "17 Years of Data" framing.

## Brand guide compliance

- Voice: leads with the takeaway, short sentences, lightly opinionated,
  pushes back on an industry habit (reacting to early winners-and-losers
  lists) with XOVI's own 2018 post as the backing.
- Key message pillars hit: data that drives profit (reading the curve for a
  client), client-proof reporting (white-label report with interpretation),
  all-in-one (OVI Analysis inside the same platform).
- No guarantees about rankings or traffic.
- No banned vocabulary. No "AI-powered", no "next-generation", no
  "game-changer".
- Competitors are never named. "Most visibility indexes swap keywords in and
  out" positions by contrast without naming anyone.
- No technical explanation of how language models work; AI Overviews are
  described only by their effect on clicks.
